Extract as many standalone, self-contained clips as the conversation genuinely supports, each between 30 and 90 seconds. Every clip must work as a complete short-form video with zero external context — a viewer with no knowledge of the guest, show, or topic should immediately understand and feel the value of the clip within the first 2-3 seconds. Prioritize: sharp opinions or contrarian takes, concrete actionable insights or frameworks, punchy one-liners or quotable moments, compelling personal stories with a clear beginning-middle-end, and moments of genuine emotion, humor, or tension. Each clip must start at a natural hook (a strong opening line, question, or statement — never mid-thought) and end at a natural resolution or punchline (never cut off mid-sentence or mid-idea). Avoid clips that reference earlier unaired parts of the conversation, use vague pronouns without antecedents (like this, that, it, he, she) referring to something not explained within the clip itself, or require the viewer to know who is speaking or why. Do not force weak moments into clips just to hit a count — only include a clip if it would genuinely stop someone mid-scroll and hold their attention for the full duration. Scan the entire transcript from start to finish and pull every moment that meets this bar, not just the first few strong ones.
